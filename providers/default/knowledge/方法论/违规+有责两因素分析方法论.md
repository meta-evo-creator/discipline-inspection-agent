# Dual-Factor (Violation + Culpability) Analysis Methodology (v2.0 Enhanced)

## ⚠️ Usage Notes

This document is the core methodology file for the DI skill, integrating the official CCDI disciplinary violation composition theory, the four-tool methodology, and **five system-thinking enhancement tools**. **Follow the "3-Stage, 11-Step + Enhancement Nodes" operational flow.**

The four-tool suite and enhancement tools are embedded as "enhancement modules" within the base workflow. They cannot independently serve as characterization grounds but substantially increase characterization accuracy and the specificity of institutional improvement recommendations.

**New in v2.0 enhancement nodes**: S1a Swiss Cheese Audit (Swiss Cheese Model), S4a Attribution Bias Calibration (Fundamental Attribution Error), S8 Just Culture Refinement (Just Culture Algorithm), S9a Scapegoat Risk Audit (Scapegoat Mechanism).

**Scope**: DI (Discipline Inspection Review). SI/HI/CA may reference and adapt these sections for analogous analysis.

## Part 1: CCDI "Violation + Culpability" Dual-Factor Analysis (Base Layer)

### 1.1 Regulatory Sources

- Article 7 of the *CPC Disciplinary Punishment Regulations*
- CCDI Case Review Office, *Introduction and Connotation of "Disciplinary Reasoning"* (China Discipline Inspection and Supervision Journal)
- *CPC Disciplinary Punishment Regulations: 100 Q&A*

### 1.2 Core Formula

> **Disciplinary Violation = Violation Elements Satisfied + Culpability Elements Satisfied + No Exculpatory Circumstances**

```
        Step 1: Violation Determination (Objective Level)
                    ↓ satisfied?
        Step 2: Culpability Determination (Subjective + Responsibility Level)
                    ↓ satisfied?
      ╔══════════════╗
      ║  Constitutes ║
      ║  Disciplinary ║
      ║  Violation   ║
      ╚══════════════╝
```

### 1.3 Violation Elements (Objective Level) — Six Items

| Element | Definition | Review Question | Evidence Requirement |
|---------|------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| **1. Actor/Subject** | The person who committed the act (Party member / public official / leading cadre / person with special status) | Is the subject qualified? Are there specific identity requirements? | Identity documentation (Party member registration form, appointment/removal documents, job description) |
| **2. Harmful Act** | The specific conduct violating Party Constitution / regulations / policies / ethics | What violation was committed? Is the cited provision correct? | Conduct records (documentary evidence, physical evidence, electronic data, witness testimony) |
| **3. Harm Object** | The protected interest violated by the conduct (Party discipline, state interests, public interests, etc.) | What protected legal interest was violated? | Object evidence (relevant regulatory provisions) |
| **4. Harm Result** | Damage or adverse impact caused | What consequences occurred? Severity level? | Result evidence (audit reports, expert opinions, social impact assessment) |
| **5. Causation** | Objective link between conduct and result | Was it caused by this conduct? Any intervening factors? | Causal chain evidence (chronological order, logical connection, exclusive evidence) |
| **6. Violation Exculpatory Circumstances** | Legitimate reasons excluding unlawfulness | Are there lawful/legitimate justifications? (Necessity, proper performance of duty, superior order, etc.) | Exculpatory evidence (approval documents, emergency situation proof) |

### 1.4 Culpability Elements (Subjective + Responsibility Level) — Five Items

| Element | Definition | Review Question | Assessment Criteria |
|---------|------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| **1. Intent** | Knowing the conduct is prohibited and either desiring or permitting the result | Did the subject know or should have known? | Direct intent (knowing + pursuing); Indirect intent (knowing + permitting) |
| **2. Negligence** | Should have foreseen the prohibited result but failed to, or carelessly believed it could be avoided | Did the subject exercise reasonable duty of care? | Careless negligence (should foresee but did not); Overconfident negligence (foresaw but carelessly believed avoidable) |
| **3. Capacity for Responsibility** | Whether the subject had the intellectual/cognitive conditions to understand and comply with discipline | Did the subject have the capacity to follow the rules? | Mental condition, cognitive level, information access conditions, institutional training received |
| **4. Purpose and Motive** | The internal driving force of the conduct | Why was this done? Self-serving or system-compelled? | Subjective statements + objective circumstantial evidence |
| **5. Culpability Exculpatory Circumstances** | Legitimate reasons not to bear responsibility | Are there force majeure, coercion, institutional compulsion or other exempting circumstances? | Force majeure evidence, coercion evidence, institutional compulsion basis |

### 1.5 Key Precedent Reference

| Case | Violation Element | Culpability Element | Conclusion | Reasoning |
|------|-------------------|---------------------|------------|-----------|
| **Failure to report personal matters (unintentional)** | ✅ Violated reporting obligation | ❌ Intent element not satisfied | **No disciplinary violation** | Omission ≠ concealment; no subjective intent |
| **Failure to report (serious circumstances, e.g., underreported residence by 50m²+)** | ✅ Violated obligation | ✅ **Presumed intent** | **Constitutes violation** | Severity of circumstances → presumed knowing and intentional |
| **Accepting public-funded banquet** | ✅ Paid with public funds | ❌ Unaware it was public funds | **No disciplinary violation** | When subjective knowledge is insufficient, objective violation alone is not enough |
| **Transferring case-related property** | ❌ Property transferred was not illicit proceeds | ✅ Collusive transfer intent | **No disciplinary violation** | Only culpability elements satisfied; violation elements not satisfied |

### 2.2 Module-Specific Operating Procedures

#### M1 — Incentive Compatibility Enhancement Module

**Function**: When determining violation elements, ask whether the system itself is a "driver" of the violation.

**Procedure**:

```
After identifying a violation, ask:
"Under the same system, would a normal person in the same position make the same mistake?"
  ├─ No → System design is basically reasonable; proceed with standard review
  └─ Yes → System has incentive incompatibility; initiate four-dimension inspection

Four-Dimension (Power-Interest-Trust-Virtue) Inspection:
  ☐ Power: Are the consequences of violation clearly foreseeable?
      Vague → Subject may misjudge risk
  ☐ Interest: Does compliance yield genuine benefits?
      Violation is more "profitable" → System is rewarding violation
  ☐ Trust: Are rules enforced consistently?
      Selective enforcement → Subject perceives outcomes as "luck"
  ☐ Virtue: Is the system shaping positive identity?
      Good actors eliminated → Lemon market activates

▶ Severe systemic deficiency → Recommend reforming the system first, then handle individuals based on circumstances
▶ Minor systemic deficiency → Note institutional recommendations; continue standard review
```

**Theoretical Origin**: Leonid Hurwicz, Mechanism Design Theory

#### M2 — Market for Lemons Enhancement Module

**Function**: When reviewing evidence, verify the signal reliability of the judgment basis.

**Procedure**:

```
Audit evidence signal strength:
  ☐ Strong signals (extremely costly to forge/imitate):
      Original bank statements, audit reports, third-party verification, timestamp data
      → Can be directly relied upon; reduce cross-verification cost

  ☐ Medium signals (forgeable but costly):
      Officially-sealed documents, surveillance footage, electronic approval records
      → Requires some cross-verification

  ☐ Weak signals (low cost to forge/imitate):
      Subject statements, witness testimony (single oral account), tip-off letters
      → Must be corroborated by evidence from 2+ independent sources
      → At least 2 independent pieces of evidence for each relevant fact

  ☐ Pseudo-signals (created specifically to satisfy review):
      Retroactively drafted meeting minutes, retroactively signed approval forms, "inspection-ready ledgers"
      → Compare deviations between "reported data" and "operational data"
      → Look for "Lao Zhou's notebook" rather than only reading "Director Liu's PowerPoint"

▶ Reviewer must be aware of potential information asymmetry:
  Am I seeing only "ledgers/documents" (Director Liu's data),
  or also "actual operations" (Lao Zhou's data)?
```

**Theoretical Origin**: George Akerlof, "The Market for Lemons" (1970)

#### M3 — Unintended Consequences Enhancement Module

**Function**: After determining the disposition plan, audit its potential side effects.

**Procedure**:

```
After finalizing disposition plan, anticipate four types of unintended consequences:

  ☐ Pattern 1: Detour
      Overly harsh punishment → Violation shifts to more concealed forms
      → Recommendation: Simultaneously strengthen monitoring of sensitive indicators

  ☐ Pattern 2: Crowding-Out Effect
      Over-reliance on punishment → Ethical self-discipline replaced by "price"
      → Recommendation: Preserve space for positive incentives

  ☐ Pattern 3: Deterioration via Substitution
      Blocking Path A → Switching to worse Path B
      → Recommendation: Investigate possible substitution paths

  ☐ Pattern 4: Temporal Front-Loading
      Campaign-style enforcement → Short-term data improvement, long-term problem resurgence
      → Recommendation: Design normalized mechanisms, avoid extreme swings

▶ Significant unintended consequence risk → Adjust disposition plan
▶ Risk is manageable → Recommend concurrent establishment of monitoring mechanisms
```

**Theoretical Origin**: Robert K. Merton, "The Unanticipated Consequences of Purposive Social Action" (1936)

#### M4 — Four-Tier Accountability Enhancement Module (Core Interface)

**Function**: Deepen culpability assessment; precisely locate the attribution tier.

**Procedure**:

```
Culpability assessment → Inquire into accountability attribution tier:

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Tier 1: Individual Fault (Personal Trouble)          │
│  ─────────────────────────────────────                │
│  Conditions: Actor has full responsibility capacity   │
│  + intent + not institutionally compelled              │
│  Accountability method: Disciplinary punishment /     │
│  judicial referral                                    │
│  Key question: Under the same system, most people     │
│  didn't do it → Lock in Tier 1                        │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Tier 2: Management Failure                           │
│  ─────────────────────────────────────                │
│  Conditions: Manager failed to fulfill institutional  │
│  + inspection/supervision obligations                 │
│  Accountability method: Admonishment talk /            │
│  organizational measures / leadership responsibility  │
│  Key question: System has provisions but execution    │
│  inadequate → Lock in Tier 2                          │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Tier 3: Systemic Flaw                                │
│  ─────────────────────────────────────                │
│  Conditions: System design has loopholes or incentive  │
│  distortions; normal people would also make mistakes  │
│  Accountability method: Rectification notice /        │
│  regulation revision / special governance              │
│  Key question: A different person would make the      │
│  same mistake → Lock in Tier 3                        │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Tier 4: Structural Dilemma                           │
│  ─────────────────────────────────────                │
│  Conditions: All parties act rationally but still      │
│  produce undesirable outcomes                         │
│  Accountability method: Policy recommendation /       │
│  systemic reform push                                 │
│  Key question: Everyone is "right" but the result     │
│  is "wrong" → No individual accountability; pursue    │
│  reform                                               │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Four Tiers → Corresponding Culpability Assessment:
  Tier 1 = Intent → Highest culpability
  Tier 2 = Negligence → Medium culpability
  Tier 3 = Systemic → Culpability transferred (partially exculpatory)
  Tier 4 = Structural → Culpability precluded (no individual liability)

▶ Attribution tier affects disposition method selection
▶ Accountability beyond Tier 1 must concurrently provide institutional improvement recommendations
```

#### M6 — Swiss Cheese Audit Enhancement Module (New in v2.0)

**Origin**: James Reason's Swiss Cheese Model — Major accidents = multiple layers of defense simultaneously perforated

**Function**: At S1 violation act determination, not only examine the "active failure" but also trace back "latent conditions."

**Procedure**:

```
During S1 violation act determination, supplement with five-layer defense audit:

Layer 5 - Cultural Layer: Does the organization tacitly condone/tolerate this type of conduct?
Layer 4 - Supervisory Layer: Who checks? What was checked? Was the check effective?
Layer 3 - Institutional Layer: Does the system itself have loopholes or incentive distortions? (Connects to M1)
Layer 2 - Management Layer: Did the manager fulfill the supervisory obligations assigned by the system? (Connects to M4 Tier 2)
Layer 1 - Operational Layer: What specifically did the actor do? (Active failure)

Output:
  "Active failure: Actor [name] made a mistake at [node] by [specific conduct]
   Latent conditions: Layers [2-5] defenses simultaneously perforated — [specific description]"
```

#### M7 — Attribution Bias Calibration Module (New in v2.0)

**Origin**: Lee Ross's Fundamental Attribution Error — Humans instinctively overestimate dispositional factors and underestimate situational factors.

**Function**: During S4 culpability determination, reviewer self-checks for attribution bias.

**Procedure**:

```
During S4 culpability determination, insert three self-checks:

☐ Self-Check 1: Am I "essentializing" the subject's conduct?
    "He's just that kind of person" → Attribution bias alert. Reframe: Under what circumstances/incentives would he act this way?

☐ Self-Check 2: Would a normal person in the same position make the same mistake?
    "Yes" → Culpability at least partially transfers to M4 Tier 3 (Systemic Flaw)
    "No" → Lock in M4 Tier 1 (Individual Fault); continue

☐ Self-Check 3: Have I adequately investigated situational evidence?
    Must retrieve: Institutional documents, job descriptions, workflows, training records
    Must not rely solely on: Subject statements, witness testimony
```

**Applicable Scenarios**: Especially when the subject has already been labeled (e.g., "stubborn," "uncooperative," "bad attitude"). Attribution calibration does not alter the violation determination but affects the culpability assessment.

#### M8 — Just Culture Refinement Module (New in v2.0)

**Origin**: David Marx's Just Culture Algorithm — Distinguish human error / at-risk behavior / reckless conduct.

**Function**: Further decompose M4 Tier 1 "Individual Fault" into three sub-tiers.

**Procedure**:

```
Decompose M4 Tier 1 into three sub-tiers:

Tier 1A - Reckless Conduct: Knowingly undertook major risk, willfully for personal gain
  → Disciplinary punishment / Judicial referral
  → Example: Accepting bribes, intentional fabrication

Tier 1B - At-Risk Behavior: Habitual deviation for shortcuts, convenience
  → Correct behavior + eliminate institutional incentives + training
  → Example: Single-person review instead of dual-person review, skipping approval signatures
  → Principle: People take shortcuts often because the system rewards taking shortcuts

Tier 1C - Human Error: Fatigue / oversight / operational mistake
  → Console + fix system + supplement training; do not pursue individual
  → Example: Misreading medication name under fatigue, missing a step during alarm response
```

**Why This Matters**: The original Tier 1's blanket "Individual Fault → Disciplinary Punishment" can lead to two types of errors: excessive harshness (punishing human errors) or excessive leniency (attributing reckless conduct to the system).

#### M9 — Scapegoat Risk Audit Module (New in v2.0)

**Origin**: Rene Girard's Scapegoat Mechanism — The scapegoat is not the cause found, but the outlet chosen.

**Function**: Add scapegoat risk self-check within S9 unintended consequences audit.

**Procedure**:

```
Within S9 unintended consequences audit, add three scapegoat audit checks:

☐ Audit 1: Does the plan satisfy the emotional need that "someone was held accountable" or the actual need that "the systemic loophole was fixed"?
    → Only person held accountable with no institutional improvement → "Suspicious plan"

☐ Audit 2: If only the person is handled and the system is not fixed, will the same problem recur within 3 months?
    → Will recur → Must supplement with institutional improvement

☐ Audit 3: Is the person held accountable truly primarily responsible, or were they most easily pushed forward because they were "closest to the accident"?
    → If the latter → Trace back to M6 Swiss Cheese Audit

▶ All three pass → Plan is reasonable. Any fails → Adjust plan; supplement institutional improvement.

Additional: After major incidents, when accountability pressure is high, scapegoat risk is highest — reviewers must consciously resist the pressure to "quickly push a scapegoat."
```

### 3.2 Step-by-Step Operating Procedures

#### Stage 1: Fact Finding (Foundation)

**S1 — Violation Act Determination**
- Identify the specific provisions allegedly violated
- Describe specific factual conduct (time, place, method, frequency, duration)
- Cross-reference specific provisions (article-section-item precise matching)
- Required tool: CA regulation database (search for original regulation text)
- Enhancement module: [Incentive Compatibility] concurrently flag whether the system may have "driven" this conduct

**S1a — Swiss Cheese Audit (New in v2.0; invokes M6)**
- Concurrently with S1 violation act determination, supplement with five-layer defense audit
- Output: Active failure + latent conditions presented side by side
- See M6 for details

**S2 — Subject Identity Confirmation**
- Confirm identity: Party member / public official / leading cadre / specialized position
- Check institutional obligations corresponding to job responsibilities
- Confirm responsibility capacity: age, mental condition, professional background

**S3 — Causation and Result Assessment**
- Confirm chronological and logical connection between conduct and result
- Exclude other intervening factors
- Quantify severity of results
- Evidence standard: For disciplinary cases, evidence from 2+ independent sources must corroborate each other

**S4 — Culpability Determination (Core)**
- Intent/Negligence assessment:
  - Knew the rules + actively violated → Intent
  - Should have known the rules + careless oversight → Negligence
  - Did not know the rules + training was adequate → Possibly exculpatory
- Purpose and motive analysis
- Severity of circumstances screening

**S4a — Attribution Bias Calibration (New in v2.0; invokes M7)**
- Concurrently with culpability determination, reviewer performs three self-checks
- See M7 for details

**S5 — Exculpatory Circumstances Review**
- Violation exculpatory: Necessity, proper performance of duty, lawful superior order
- Culpability exculpatory: Force majeure, coercion, involuntariness, institutional compulsion

#### Stage 2: Enhanced Analysis (Method)

**S6 — Incentive Compatibility Enhancement (Institutional Source Tracing)**
- Inquire whether institutional incentives are problematic
- Apply the Power-Interest-Trust-Virtue four-dimension framework
- Output: Institutional deficiency assessment (None / Minor / Severe)

**S7 — Market for Lemons Enhancement (Signal Audit)**
- Evidence signal strength rating (Strong / Medium / Weak / Pseudo)
- Weak signals require 2+ independent sources
- Output: Evidence reliability assessment matrix

**S8 — Four-Tier Accountability Enhancement + Just Culture Refinement (Responsibility Attribution Positioning; invokes M4 + M8)**
- Align culpability assessment with the four accountability tiers
- If attributed to Tier 1, activate M8 Just Culture Refinement:
  - Tier 1A (Reckless) → Disciplinary punishment
  - Tier 1B (At-Risk) → Correct behavior + reform system
  - Tier 1C (Human Error) → Fix system; do not pursue individual
- Output: Attribution tier + Just Culture classification + punishment recommendation

#### Stage 3: Conclusion Output (Result)

**S9 — Unintended Consequences Audit + Scapegoat Risk Audit (Invokes M3 + M9)**
- Anticipate four types of unintended consequences
- Append M9 Scapegoat Audit: three self-checks
- Output: Risk warnings + Scapegoat risk conclusion

**S10 — Composite Conclusion and Disposition Plan**
- Disciplinary violation factual conclusion
- Attribution tier conclusion
- Disposition recommendation

**S11 — Institutional Improvement Recommendations Output**
- Tier 3 → Direction for institutional revision
- Tier 4 → Systemic reform recommendations
- Tier 2 → Management mechanism optimization
- Tier 1 → Prevention and control recommendations

### 4.3 Division of Labor with CA

| Dimension | CA (Compliance Analysis) | DI (Discipline Inspection) |
|-----------|--------------------------|----------------------------|
| Timing | Ex-ante | Ex-post |
| Core Question | "Can this be done?" | "How is this characterized once done?" |
| Basis | Original regulation text + compliance obligations | Original regulation text + violation + culpability + attribution |
| Enhancement Modules | May reference Incentive Compatibility / Market for Lemons thinking | Full workflow: nine-module integration |
| Output | Compliance determination + risk level | Violation characterization + attribution tier + disposition plan + institutional recommendations |

### 4.4 Ethical Boundaries

1. **DI cannot substitute for judicial adjudication**: Once significant cases are referred to judicial authorities, judicial findings take precedence.
2. **Dual-factor cross-verification**: Institutional deficiency assessments from enhancement modules cannot serve as the sole basis for exemption.
3. **Evidence exclusion rules**: Weak/Pseudo signal evidence must not independently serve as the basis for characterization.
4. **Fault tolerance and appeal**: Any disposition plan must preserve the subject's right to defense and appeal channels.
5. **Transparent and traceable**: All determination processes must be recorded and preserved for subsequent audit.
